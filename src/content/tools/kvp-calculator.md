## Understanding Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP 2019)

**Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP)** is a popular small savings instrument backed by the **Government of India (Ministry of Finance)** and sold through **India Post** offices and designated commercial banks. It is designed for conservative investors seeking a 100% sovereign-backed, risk-free instrument that doubles their capital over a specified timeframe.

---

## Key Statutory Features of KVP

| Feature Parameter | Statutory Provision & Scheme Rules |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Current Interest Rate** | **7.5% p.a.** (notified for FY 2024-25 Q4) |
| **Compounding Frequency** | **Compounded Annually** |
| **Statutory Doubling Period** | **Exactly 115 Months** (9 Years and 7 Months) |
| **Maturity Payout** | **Exactly 2x Principal Deposit** ($2 \times P$) |
| **Minimum Deposit Limit** | **₹1,000** (in multiples of ₹100) |
| **Maximum Deposit Limit** | **No Upper Ceiling** (Uncapped investment) |
| **Statutory Lock-in Period** | **2.5 Years (30 Months)** |
| **Section 80C Benefit** | **Not Eligible** for 80C deduction |
| **Sovereign Guarantee** | **100% Government of India Guarantee** |

## Maturity Calculation & Doubling Timeline

$$\text{Maturity Payout} = 2 \times \text{Principal Deposit}$$

$$\text{Total Interest Earned} = \text{Principal Deposit}$$

For example, a deposit of **₹1,00,000** doubles into **₹2,00,000** at Month 115 (9 years 7 months), generating **₹1,00,000** in total compound interest.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. How is interest taxed in Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP)?
KVP interest is taxed annually as income under the head "Income from Other Sources" according to your income tax slab rate. No TDS is deducted by India Post.

### 2. Can KVP certificates be transferred to another person?
Yes. KVP certificates can be transferred from one individual to another, or from one post office to another, subject to official Post Office transfer applications.

### 3. Can I pledge KVP certificates as collateral for a bank loan?
Yes. KVP certificates are accepted by commercial banks and financial institutions as collateral security for securing loans.
