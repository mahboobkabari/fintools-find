## Mastering Working Capital Management & Corporate Liquidity

In corporate finance and treasury operations, **Working Capital** represents the lifeblood of day-to-day commercial activities. It measures a business's operational liquidity, short-term solvency, and capacity to satisfy immediate obligations—such as supplier invoices, employee payroll, and bank credit facilities—without experiencing financial distress.

---

### Core Working Capital & Liquidity Formulations

| Financial Metric | Mathematical Formula | Institutional Benchmark | Key Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Net Working Capital (NWC)** | $\text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$ | Positive Surplus | Operational buffer funding ongoing production and payroll. |
| **Current Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ | $1.5\text{x} - 2.0\text{x}$ | General solvency; $< 1.0\text{x}$ indicates immediate liquidity deficit. |
| **Quick (Acid-Test) Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Cash} + \text{Accounts Receivable}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ | $\ge 1.0\text{x}$ | Immediate liquidity without relying on inventory liquidation. |
| **Cash Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Cash \& Marketable Securities}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ | $0.2\text{x} - 0.5\text{x}$ | Pure cash coverage against short-term debt and creditors. |
| **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** | $\text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO}$ | $30 - 60 \text{ Days}$ | Net velocity of cash through raw material, inventory, and sales cycles. |

---

### The Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Breakdown

The **Cash Conversion Cycle** measures the time elapsed from the moment cash is deployed to purchase inventory until cash is collected from customer sales:

$$\text{Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)} = \left(\frac{\text{Accounts Receivable}}{\text{Annual Revenue}}\right) \times 365$$

$$\text{Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)} = \left(\frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{Annual COGS}}\right) \times 365$$

$$\text{Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)} = \left(\frac{\text{Accounts Payable}}{\text{Annual COGS}}\right) \times 365$$

$$\text{Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO}$$

By compressing DSO (faster billing) and DIO (lean inventory) while negotiating extended DPO (favorable supplier terms), businesses unlock substantial cash and eliminate costly bank overdraft interest.
