## Understanding Current Ratio in Balance Sheet Solvency

The Current Ratio is the foundation of short-term solvency analysis in corporate finance:

$$\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Current Assets}}{\text{Total Current Liabilities}}$$

$$\text{Net Working Capital (NWC)} = \text{Total Current Assets} - \text{Total Current Liabilities}$$

### Working Capital Ratio Interpretation Matrix

| Current Ratio | Solvency Status | Commercial Underwriting Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **< 1.00x** | **Working Capital Deficit** | Severe liquidity risk; liabilities exceed liquid assets. |
| **1.00x – 1.49x** | **Tight Buffer** | Meets obligations but vulnerable to customer delays or supplier demands. |
| **1.50x – 2.50x** | **Optimal Institutional Standard** | Healthy cushion; comfortable compliance with bank covenants. |
| **> 3.00x** | **Excess Idle Capital** | Sub-optimal cash utilization; potential excess slow-moving inventory. |
