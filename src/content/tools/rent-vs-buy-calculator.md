## Understanding Rent vs Buy Housing Decisions

Deciding whether to buy a house or rent and invest the capital difference is one of the most critical personal finance decisions. While homeownership offers security and equity build-up, renting provides flexibility and preserves capital for high-yield equity investments.

A complete **Rent vs Buy Decision Engine** does not merely compare monthly EMI against monthly rent. It models the **30-year multi-asset net worth trajectory** of both choices.

## Rent vs Buy Financial Formulas

### 1. Buy Scenario Net Worth ($NW_{\text{Buy}}(y)$)
$$NW_{\text{Buy}}(y) = V_{\text{property}}(y) - \text{LoanBalance}(y) - C_{\text{selling}}(y) + \text{TaxBenefits}(y)$$

Where:
- $V_{\text{property}}(y) = \text{PropertyPrice} \times (1 + g_{\text{property}})^y$
- $C_{\text{selling}}(y) = V_{\text{property}}(y) \times \text{SellingCost\%}$

### 2. Rent Scenario Net Worth ($NW_{\text{Rent}}(y)$)
$$NW_{\text{Rent}}(y) = V_{\text{Lumpsum}}(y) + V_{\text{SIPSurplus}}(y)$$

Where:
- $V_{\text{Lumpsum}}(y) = \text{InitialCashBuy} \times (1 + r_{\text{equity}})^y$
- Initial Cash Buy = $\text{DownPayment} + \text{PurchaseTransactionCosts}$
- $V_{\text{SIPSurplus}}(y)$ = Accumulated value of monthly surplus $(\text{EMI} + \text{Maintenance}) - \text{Rent}$ invested @ $r_{\text{equity}}\%$

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. How does the Rent vs Buy Calculator determine the winner?
The calculator compares the estimated net worth built in Year $N$ by buying property (property market value minus loan balance and selling costs) against renting (accumulated equity portfolio built from initial down payment lumpsum + monthly cash savings). The option yielding higher net worth is recommended.

### 2. What is Section 24(b) tax benefit?
Under the Old Tax Regime (AY 2025-26), house owners can deduct up to ₹2 Lakhs p.a. of home loan interest from their taxable income for self-occupied properties. This reduces annual tax liability for buyers.

### 3. What is the impact of rental yield?
Rental yield is annual rent divided by property price ($\text{Rent} \times 12 / \text{Price}$). In markets with low rental yields (2%-3%), renting is often more cost-effective because the difference between EMI and Rent can be invested in higher-yielding equity assets.
